<h1 dir="ltr">How to Choose a General Contractor in Edmonton: 10 Questions to Ask Before You Sign</h1>
<hr />
<p dir="ltr">To choose a <a href="https://www.belvanconstruction.com/">general contractor Edmonton</a> homeowners and business owners can trust, verify four things first: a valid business licence and the right trade credentials, active liability insurance, current WCB Alberta coverage, and references from recent projects similar to yours. Then compare itemized quotes, confirm who manages the subcontractors, and make sure the contract spells out scope, schedule, payment milestones, and change-order rules.</p>
<p dir="ltr">The rest of this guide covers each check in detail, as ten questions you can ask in any first meeting.</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">What Does a General Contractor Actually Manage?</h3>
<p dir="ltr">A general contractor is the single party responsible for delivering your project. That includes hiring and scheduling subcontractors (framers, electricians, plumbers, finishers), ordering materials, applying for permits, meeting building code, and coordinating inspections. You deal with one accountable point of contact instead of managing every trade yourself.</p>
<p dir="ltr">This is why the choice matters. Their scheduling, supplier relationships, and subcontractor quality directly set your timeline, budget, and finished result.</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Question 1: Are You Licensed, and What Does Your Licence Cover?</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Ask for the contractor&rsquo;s City of Edmonton business licence and any trade or builder credentials relevant to your project. Alberta requires new home builders to hold a provincial builder licence, while renovation and commercial work are governed by municipal licensing, safety codes permits, and trade certifications for the specific work involved (electrical, gas, plumbing).</p>
<p dir="ltr">Don&rsquo;t accept a verbal &ldquo;yes.&rdquo; Ask for the licence number and check it yourself.</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Question 2: Do You Carry Liability Insurance and WCB Coverage?</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Request a current certificate of insurance showing commercial general liability coverage, and ask for the policy limits. Then ask for a WCB Alberta clearance letter, which confirms the contractor&rsquo;s account is in good standing.</p>
<p dir="ltr">This is the question that protects you most. If an uninsured worker is injured on your property or a subcontractor damages a neighbouring structure, the financial exposure can fall on the property owner. Reputable contractors send these documents without hesitation.</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Question 3: Can I See Recent Projects Similar to Mine?</h3>
<p dir="ltr">A contractor who builds beautiful custom kitchens may not be the right fit for a commercial tenant improvement. Ask for completed projects of similar size, scope, and building type, ideally within the last two to three years.</p>
<p dir="ltr">Ask for photos, but more importantly ask for two or three client references you can call. When you call, ask whether the project finished on time, how surprises were handled, and whether the final cost matched the contract.</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Question 4: How Is Your Quote Structured, and What&rsquo;s Excluded?</h3>
<p dir="ltr">A trustworthy estimate is itemized. It separates labour, materials, subcontractor costs, permit fees, overhead, and profit, and it states allowances (placeholder amounts for items you haven&rsquo;t chosen yet, like tile or light fixtures).</p>
<p dir="ltr">The lowest number on a quote often hides exclusions. Ask directly: &ldquo;What isn&rsquo;t included in this price?&rdquo; Common gaps are demolition disposal, permit fees, engineering, site cleanup, and finishing work. If you&rsquo;re weighing a fixed-price contract against cost-plus, ask which one the contractor recommends and why.</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Question 5: Who Will Be on Site, and Who Manages Them?</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Find out whether the contractor uses in-house crews, long-term subcontractors, or whoever is available that month. Also ask who your day-to-day contact is, such as a project manager or site supervisor, and how often they&rsquo;ll be on site.</p>
<p dir="ltr">Stable subcontractor relationships usually mean better scheduling and more consistent quality. Constant crew turnover is a common source of delays.</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Question 6: Who Pulls the Permits, and Do You Handle Inspections?</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Permits protect you. In Edmonton, structural, electrical, plumbing, gas, and many renovation projects require permits under Alberta&rsquo;s Safety Codes system, with inspections at set stages.</p>
<p dir="ltr">A professional contractor applies for permits in their own name, schedules inspections, and passes on the paperwork. Be cautious if a contractor suggests skipping a permit to save money. Unpermitted work can cause trouble with insurance claims, resale, and future inspections.</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Question 7: What&rsquo;s Your Realistic Timeline, and What Could Change It?</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Ask for a written schedule with milestones, not just a finish date. A good contractor will also explain what could shift it: permit review times, material lead times, weather, and inspection availability.</p>
<p dir="ltr">Edmonton&rsquo;s climate is a real factor. Concrete work, exterior framing, roofing, and site work are affected by cold temperatures and frost, so ask how the schedule accounts for seasonal conditions. Projects started in winter often need cold-weather measures such as heated enclosures and concrete additives, and these should be reflected in the plan and the price.</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Question 8: How Do You Handle Change Orders and Unexpected Costs?</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Almost every project hits surprises: hidden water damage, outdated wiring, unsuitable soil. What separates good contractors is the process, not the absence of surprises.</p>
<p dir="ltr">Ask how changes are documented. The standard is a written change order that states the cost and schedule impact and requires your signature before the work proceeds. Also ask how much contingency they recommend. Ten to fifteen percent is common on renovations, since older Edmonton homes can hide a lot behind the walls.</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Question 9: What Warranty Do You Provide?</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Ask what&rsquo;s covered, for how long, and who handles callbacks. Workmanship warranties commonly run one to two years, and manufacturer warranties on materials run separately. For new homes, Alberta&rsquo;s mandatory home warranty requirements apply, so ask how that coverage is arranged.</p>
<p dir="ltr">Also ask how a warranty request is handled in practice: who you call, and how quickly they respond.</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Question 10: What Does the Contract Say About Payment, Holdbacks, and Liens?</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Read the contract before signing, not after. It should clearly state:</p>
<ul dir="ltr">
<li>The full scope of work and what&rsquo;s excluded</li>
<li>The total price or the cost-plus formula</li>
<li>A payment schedule tied to completed milestones, not large upfront deposits</li>
<li>Start and completion dates</li>
<li>Change-order procedures</li>
<li>Holdback terms under Alberta&rsquo;s Prompt Payment and Construction Lien Act</li>
<li>Dispute resolution and termination terms</li>
</ul>
<p dir="ltr">Under Alberta&rsquo;s lien legislation, owners generally hold back a percentage of payments for a set period to protect against unpaid subcontractor claims. Ask the contractor to explain how this works on your project and to confirm that subcontractors and suppliers are being paid. If anything in the contract is unclear, a lawyer can review it before you sign.</p>
<h3 dir="ltr">Warning Signs to Watch For</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Slow down if you notice any of these:</p>
<ul dir="ltr">
<li>Pressure to sign the same day, or &ldquo;today only&rdquo; pricing</li>
<li>Large cash deposits or requests to pay in cash</li>
<li>No written contract, or a vague one</li>
<li>A quote far below every other bid</li>
<li>Reluctance to provide insurance, WCB, or reference details</li>
<li>No physical address or business history you can verify</li>
</ul>
<h3 dir="ltr">How to Compare Your Shortlist</h3>
<p dir="ltr">Collect three itemized quotes, then compare them on the same basis: scope, allowances, exclusions, timeline, and warranty. Weigh communication quality too. The contractor who answers questions clearly and promptly during the quoting stage is usually the one who will keep you informed during construction.</p>
<p dir="ltr">Price matters, but the best value is the contractor who finishes the job correctly, on schedule, and without disputes.</p>
